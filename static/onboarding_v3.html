Methodology

Why This Works

The story felt casual. The science behind it is anything but. Here's what each chapter was actually measuring.

When people know they're being assessed, they perform. This is called social desirability bias.

A story bypasses this. You engage System 1 thinking (fast, instinctive, honest) rather than System 2 (slow, analytical, performative).

Grounded in scenario-based preference elicitation from behavioural economics.

Chapter 1 — Who's joining you?
Social context & format preference. Solo → focused dining. Date → higher spend.

Chapter 2 — How are you feeling?
Mood-driven adventurousness. "Exhausted" → familiar. "Celebratory" → fine dining.

Chapter 3 — What did you search?
Local engagement & adventurousness proxy. Search behaviour is honest.

Chapter 4 — The 45-minute wait.
Patience/quality signal. Wait tolerance correlates with price orientation.

Chapter 5 — The specials board.
Highest-signal: spice, health, adventure, complexity, local engagement — all from one food choice.

Chapter 6 — The bill.
Price ceiling calibration. $95 for two is a concrete anchor.

Each taste dimension scores 0–10. The top 2–3 dimensions determine your archetype:

The Explorer — adventurousness ≥ 7 + local_engagement ≥ 6.
The Connoisseur — price ≥ 7 + complexity ≥ 7.
The Social Feaster — social ≥ 7.
The Health Seeker — health ≥ 7.
The Comfort Lover — adventure ≤ 4 + health ≤ 4.
The Value Hunter — price ≤ 3.
The Balanced Eater — no dominant dimension.
